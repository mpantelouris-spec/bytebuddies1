BYTEBUDDIES ROBOT STUDIO — COMPLETE PRIMARY SCHOOL REBUILD PROMPT

You are redesigning and rebuilding the entire Robot Studio experience for primary-school children aged 7–11.

The current robots and arenas look unfinished, confusing, empty, and visually inconsistent. Replace the experience with a polished, joyful, safe, highly accessible educational game that children immediately understand and want to replay.

The final product should feel like a premium children’s console game: colorful, expressive, responsive, playful, welcoming, and professionally designed. Use an original visual identity inspired by the clarity, charm, energy, and accessibility of family-friendly console games. Do not copy Nintendo characters, names, logos, sounds, environments, UI, or copyrighted artwork.

The product must be suitable for:
- Primary-school classrooms
- Home learning
- Teacher demonstrations
- Group activities
- Children aged 7–11
- Children with different reading abilities
- Children with color-vision differences
- Children using keyboard, mouse, touchscreen, or controller
- Children who are new to coding and games

The experience must never feel frightening, aggressive, complicated, unsafe, or embarrassing for children.

## 1. MAIN EDUCATIONAL PURPOSE

Robot Studio should teach children through play.

Every arena must help children practise:

- Sequencing
- Instructions
- Cause and effect
- Events
- Loops
- Conditions
- Debugging
- Problem solving
- Planning
- Spatial awareness
- Direction and movement
- Teamwork
- Patience
- Testing and iteration
- Safe digital behaviour
- Creative design
- Reflection on mistakes

The child should always understand:

- What am I trying to do?
- Which robot am I controlling?
- What can this robot do?
- What should I try next?
- How do I know whether it worked?
- What happens if I make a mistake?
- How can I try again?

Never require children to read long paragraphs during gameplay. Use short sentences, icons, narration, animations, arrows, demonstrations, and optional teacher explanations.

## 2. DESIGN PRINCIPLES FOR PRIMARY CHILDREN

Use these principles everywhere:

- One clear goal at a time
- Large buttons
- Large readable text
- Simple vocabulary
- Friendly visual instructions
- Immediate feedback
- No unnecessary menus
- No hidden controls
- No unexplained icons
- No confusing abbreviations
- No punishment for experimenting
- Unlimited retries in learning modes
- Encouraging messages after mistakes
- Clear progress indicators
- Consistent controls across all arenas
- Predictable robot behaviour
- Short play sessions of 3–8 minutes
- Optional advanced challenges for faster learners
- Teacher-controlled classroom settings

Use language such as:

- “Try again”
- “You’re close”
- “Let’s test it”
- “What could we change?”
- “Great idea”
- “The robot reached the wall”
- “The ball is over there”

Do not use language such as:

- “You failed”
- “Wrong”
- “Game over forever”
- “You are bad at coding”
- “Eliminated” in beginner modes
- “Critical error”
- “Invalid syntax”
- “You lost because you were too slow”

## 7. SAFE PHYSICS AND COLLISION

The physics must feel playful and predictable.

Robots must:

- Stand firmly on the ground
- Stop at walls
- Slide safely on special surfaces
- Bounce gently from soft barriers
- Avoid falling outside the level
- Recover from unusual positions
- Respect object weight
- Push only objects they are strong enough to move
- Use ramps correctly
- Detect hazards before entering them
- Return to checkpoints when needed

Every visible object that looks solid must have correct collision.

Every invisible collision must be avoided unless it protects the child from leaving the arena.

If a robot becomes stuck for more than two seconds:

- Show a gentle recovery animation
- Display “Let’s move you back to the checkpoint”
- Return the robot safely
- Preserve the child’s progress
- Never punish the child
