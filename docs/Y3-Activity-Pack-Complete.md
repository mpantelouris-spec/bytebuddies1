### Lesson 1.1 — What is a Computer?
**Activity Name:** Computer Part Relay Race
**Format:** Physical Game
**Time:** 12 minutes
**Group Size:** Two teams of ~15 children
**Placement in Lesson:** Hook (before platform activities)

**Materials Needed:**
- 12 A4 cards with computer part names (2x Monitor, 2x Keyboard, 2x Mouse, 2x CPU, 2x Speaker, 2x Webcam)
- 12 A4 cards with matching descriptions ("Shows pictures and text", "You type on this", etc.)
- 2 hula hoops or taped circles on the floor (one per team)
- Whistle or bell

**Learning Connection:**
This activity pre-teaches the vocabulary (Monitor, Keyboard, Mouse, CPU, Speaker, Webcam) and their functions before children encounter them in platform activity 1.1.A (Parts of a Computer Labelling Game). The relay format creates muscle-memory associations between part names and descriptions through physical movement, reinforcing INPUT vs OUTPUT concepts (1.1.C) kinaesthetically.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Divide class into two teams standing in lines at one end of the hall/classroom.
2. Scatter the 6 PART NAME cards face-up in Team A's hula hoop; scatter 6 DESCRIPTION cards face-up in Team B's hoop (both hoops at the opposite end of the room).
3. On "GO!", the first child from each team runs to their hoop, picks up ONE card, runs back, and tags the next teammate.
4. After all cards are collected, teams have 60 seconds to match each part to its description on the floor.
5. Beemo (teacher in role or puppet) checks: "Beemo scans your matches... BEEP BOOP!" Award 1 point per correct match.
6. Swap hoops and repeat — teams that had parts now have descriptions and vice versa.

**Mascot Tie-In:**
Introduce Beemo at the start: "Beemo the Robot has forgotten which parts do what! Can you help Beemo remember before the platform activities?"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Pre-teach the 6 parts with a quick flashcard drill; pair struggling readers with confident partners.
- Extension: Add 2 extra "tricky" parts (RAM, Hard Drive) for fast-finishing teams to match.

**Safety/Practical Notes:**
Clear a wide running lane; remind children to walk on the return if the space is tight. Ensure no loose rugs or obstacles.

### Lesson 1.2 — Algorithms: Giving Instructions
**Activity Name:** Beemo's Blindfold Walk
**Format:** Role-Play / Physical Game
**Time:** 15 minutes
**Group Size:** Pairs (Programmer + Robot)
**Placement in Lesson:** Between platform activities (after 1.2.A Morning Routine Sequencer, before 1.2.B Robot Sandwich)

**Materials Needed:**
- 15 blindfolds (fabric strips or sleep masks)
- 30 cones/chairs to create a simple obstacle course
- Large "FINISH" sign
- Vocabulary cards: ALGORITHM, SEQUENCE, PRECISE (displayed on whiteboard)

**Learning Connection:**
This activity directly reinforces the vocabulary words **Algorithm** (step-by-step instructions), **Sequence** (correct order), and **Precise** (exact and clear) by forcing children to give instructions that are literally followed. It builds on the "Make Me a Sandwich" hook and prepares children for 1.2.B Robot Sandwich by making them experience why precise, sequenced instructions matter.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Partner A is the "Programmer" (can see), Partner B is "Beemo the Robot" (blindfolded).
2. The Robot must navigate from START to FINISH using ONLY these commands: "Step forward", "Stop", "Turn left", "Turn right" (90° turns only).
3. Programmer gives ONE instruction at a time; Robot performs it, then waits.
4. If Robot bumps an obstacle, they freeze and say "ERROR! BUG DETECTED!" — Programmer must debug by giving a correcting instruction.
5. Time each pair; fastest 3 pairs win "Precise Programmer" stickers.
6. Swap roles and repeat with a slightly different course.

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"You ARE Beemo! Robots only follow exact instructions — if your Programmer says 'go over there,' you won't understand. You need PRECISE, SEQUENCED ALGORITHMS!"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Reduce obstacles to 3; allow Programmer to hold Robot's shoulders (not push, just guide).
- Extension: Add diagonal turns ("Turn half-left") or multi-step commands ("Step forward 3 times").

**Safety/Practical Notes:**
Ensure blindfolds are loose enough to remove easily; Robot can remove blindfold immediately if uncomfortable. Spotter (third child or TA) stands nearby each pair.

### Lesson 1.3 — Debugging: Finding and Fixing Mistakes
**Activity Name:** Bug Hunt Bingo
**Format:** Board Game (Bingo style)
**Time:** 10 minutes
**Group Size:** Individual with whole-class calling
**Placement in Lesson:** Plenary-consolidation (after all platform activities)

**Materials Needed:**
- 30 printed Bingo cards (5x5 grids, each cell has a "buggy instruction" like "Put toothpaste on brush BEFORE picking up brush")
- 30 whiteboard markers or counters (5 per child)
- Teacher's "Bug Caller" list of 25 buggy instructions with their fixes
- Vocabulary cards displayed: BUG, DEBUG, TEST

**Learning Connection:**
This consolidates vocabulary **Bug** (a mistake), **Debug** (find and fix), and **Test** (try it out) by requiring children to identify bugs quickly. It mirrors 1.3.A Bug Hunt and 1.3.C Debug the Recipe but in a competitive, game-based format that rewards fast recognition of sequencing errors.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Distribute Bingo cards and 5 counters to each child.
2. Teacher (as Beemo's voice) reads a buggy instruction: "Beemo tried: Put shoes on, THEN put socks on. BEEP BOOP — something's wrong!"
3. Children scan their card for that bug. If they have it, they cover it with a counter AND must whisper the fix to a partner ("Put socks on FIRST, then shoes").
4. First child to get 5 in a row shouts "DEBUGGED!" and reads out their 5 bugs + fixes to win.
5. Validate answers; if any fix is wrong, play continues.
6. Play 2-3 rounds with card swaps.

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"Beemo keeps making silly mistakes! Every time you find a bug on your card, you're helping Beemo debug. Beemo says THANK YOU in binary: 01010100 01001000 01000001 01001110 01001011 01010011!"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Provide a "Bug Fix Bank" sheet with possible fixes children can reference.
- Extension: Children create 3 new buggy instructions for next lesson's Bingo cards.

**Safety/Practical Notes:**
None beyond standard classroom supervision.

### Lesson 1.4 — Patterns and Loops
**Activity Name:** Human Loop Machine
**Format:** Music & Movement
**Time:** 12 minutes
**Group Size:** Whole class in a circle
**Placement in Lesson:** Hook (before platform activities, extending the clap-pattern challenge)

**Materials Needed:**
- Drum or tambourine (teacher)
- 4 large action cards: CLAP, STAMP, JUMP, SPIN
- Whiteboard with "REPEAT [ ] TIMES { }" written large
- Vocabulary cards: LOOP, REPEAT, PATTERN

**Learning Connection:**
This activity embodies the vocabulary **Loop** (instructions that repeat), **Repeat**, and **Pattern** physically before children encounter 1.4.B Loop Builder (comparing 8 blocks vs. 2 blocks inside REPEAT 4). By performing "REPEAT 4 TIMES { Clap, Stamp, Jump }" with their bodies, children internalise why loops are more efficient than listing every action separately.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Children stand in a circle. Teacher holds up CLAP card — everyone claps once. STAMP — everyone stamps. Practise each action.
2. Teacher writes on board: "REPEAT 4 TIMES { Clap, Clap, Stamp }" and demonstrates slowly while counting "1... 2... 3... 4..."
3. Whole class performs together with drum beat keeping time.
4. Ask: "How many total actions did we do?" (12 — but we only WROTE 3 actions inside the loop!)
5. Rotate through the circle: each child suggests a 2-3 action pattern and a repeat number (max 5). Class performs their loop.
6. Final challenge: Teacher writes "REPEAT ? TIMES { Jump, Spin }" — class performs until teacher shouts "STOP!" Children guess how many repeats happened.

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"Beemo loves dancing but hates writing long algorithms. Loops let Beemo write just a few actions and repeat them — that's the robot way!"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Limit to 2 actions per loop; use only CLAP and STAMP.
- Extension: Introduce nested loops: "REPEAT 2 TIMES { REPEAT 3 TIMES { Clap }, Jump }".

**Safety/Practical Notes:**
Ensure space for jumping; children with mobility needs can do seated actions (clap, wave, tap desk).

### Lesson 1.5 — Decisions: If This, Then That
**Activity Name:** Beemo's Choice Cards
**Format:** Card Game / Role-Play
**Time:** 15 minutes
**Group Size:** Small groups of 4
**Placement in Lesson:** Between platform activities (after 1.5.A Decision Tree Game, before 1.5.C Write Your Own IF/THEN)

**Materials Needed:**
- 40 "Condition" cards (e.g., "It is raining", "You are hungry", "It is your birthday", "The light is red")
- 40 matching "Action" cards (e.g., "Take an umbrella", "Eat a snack", "Blow out candles", "Stop walking")
- 10 "ELSE" wild cards (child invents the alternative action)
- Vocabulary cards: CONDITION, SELECTION, IF/THEN displayed prominently

**Learning Connection:**
This directly reinforces **Condition** (something that can be true or false), **Selection** (choosing based on a condition), and **IF/THEN** logic from 1.5.B Traffic Light Conditionals. Children practise constructing complete IF/THEN/ELSE statements verbally before writing them in 1.5.C.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Shuffle Condition cards into one pile, Action cards into another. Each group gets 8 Conditions and 8 Actions.
2. Turn over one Condition card and one Action card. Group decides: "Does this Action match this Condition?"
3. If yes, one child says aloud: "IF [condition] THEN [action]." — Group earns 1 point.
4. If no (e.g., Condition: "It is raining", Action: "Put on sunscreen"), group can either:
   - Discard both (no points)
   - Use an ELSE wild card: "IF it is raining THEN take umbrella, ELSE [put on sunscreen]" — 2 points!
5. Play until all cards used. Highest-scoring group shares their best IF/THEN/ELSE statement with the class.
6. Beemo's Challenge: Teacher draws one Condition; groups race to write a matching IF/THEN/ELSE on whiteboards.

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"Beemo makes decisions all day! IF battery low THEN charge, ELSE keep playing. Help Beemo learn more decisions!"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Pre-match some obvious pairs; provide sentence stems ("IF ___ THEN ___").
- Extension: Add "AND" condition cards requiring two conditions to be true.

**Safety/Practical Notes:**
None beyond standard classroom supervision.

### Lesson 2.1 — Decomposition: Breaking Problems Down
**Activity Name:** Pizza Party Planner Race
**Format:** Physical Game / Group Challenge
**Time:** 15 minutes
**Group Size:** Small groups of 5
**Placement in Lesson:** Hook (before platform activity 2.1.A Pizza Problem)

**Materials Needed:**
- 6 large "Pizza Party" poster sheets (one per group)
- 36 sticky notes per group (6 different colours)
- Marker pens
- Timer
- Vocabulary cards: DECOMPOSITION, SUB-TASK displayed

**Learning Connection:**
This activity pre-teaches **Decomposition** (breaking into smaller parts) and **Sub-task** before the platform activity by having children physically create sub-task sticky notes. The 90-second race mirrors 2.1.C Decomposition Challenge, building competitive energy.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Explain: "Pixel needs to plan a pizza party but doesn't know where to start! We need to DECOMPOSE the big problem into smaller SUB-TASKS."
2. Each group has a poster and sticky notes. Big problem written in centre: "HAVE A PIZZA PARTY."
3. Round 1 (90 seconds): Groups write as many sub-tasks as possible on separate sticky notes, arranged around the centre.
4. Timer stops. Groups count sticky notes. Highest count shares their sub-tasks.
5. Round 2: Groups must now put their sticky notes in SEQUENCE ORDER (what happens first, second, etc.) using arrows drawn between them.
6. Gallery walk: groups visit other posters and add one sticky note sub-task they didn't think of (different colour).

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"Pixel says: 'I could never plan a party alone — it's too BIG! But when you break it into tiny pieces, even a cat can do it!'"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Provide 5 "starter" sub-tasks already written; children add 5 more.
- Extension: Groups identify sub-tasks that could be done AT THE SAME TIME (parallel processing intro).

**Safety/Practical Notes:**
None beyond standard classroom supervision.

### Lesson 2.2 — Pattern Recognition
**Activity Name:** Pixel's Pattern Detective Game
**Format:** Board Game / Card Game
**Time:** 12 minutes
**Group Size:** Pairs
**Placement in Lesson:** Between platform activities (after Pattern Gallery, before Password Pattern Activity)

**Materials Needed:**
- 30 "Pattern Cards" (each showing a sequence with a missing element, e.g., 🔴🔵🔴🔵🔴❓)
- 30 "Answer Cards" (showing the missing element)
- 15 "Predict the Next 3" challenge cards
- Score sheets
- Vocabulary: PATTERN, PREDICT displayed

**Learning Connection:**
This reinforces **Pattern** (something that repeats regularly) and **Predict** (using patterns to guess what comes next), directly mirroring "Predict the Next" from the platform activities. The card game format adds tactile engagement and peer competition.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Pairs sit facing each other. Pattern Cards face-down between them.
2. One partner flips a Pattern Card. Both race to find the matching Answer Card from the spread.
3. First to slap the correct Answer Card AND say what the pattern rule is ("It's red-blue repeating") wins the pair.
4. After 10 rounds, introduce "Predict the Next 3" cards — harder sequences where children must predict 3 missing elements.
5. Fastest pair to complete all 15 challenge cards with correct predictions wins "Pattern Detective" badges.

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"Pixel has magical pattern-vision — she can spot patterns anywhere! Can you train your brain to see patterns like Pixel?"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Start with simple AB patterns only; reduce to "Predict the Next 1."
- Extension: Include number patterns (2, 4, 6, 8, ?) and growing patterns (1, 2, 4, 7, 11, ?).

**Safety/Practical Notes:**
None beyond standard classroom supervision.

### Lesson 2.3 — Abstraction: What's Important?
**Activity Name:** Map Makers Challenge
**Format:** Craft / Design
**Time:** 15 minutes
**Group Size:** Individual
**Placement in Lesson:** Hook (before platform activity "Design a Map")

**Materials Needed:**
- A4 paper (1 per child)
- Coloured pencils
- Example: a detailed photograph of a bedroom vs. a simple map of the same bedroom
- Vocabulary cards: ABSTRACTION, SIMPLIFY displayed

**Learning Connection:**
This activity teaches **Abstraction** (keeping only important information) and **Simplify** by having children create a map of the classroom using ONLY 10 features. The constraint forces abstraction decisions: "Is the plant important? Is every chair important?"

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Show the photograph vs. map comparison. Ask: "Which helps you navigate better?" Discuss why the map works — it ABSTRACTS away unnecessary details.
2. Challenge: "You have 5 minutes to draw a map of THIS CLASSROOM. But here's the rule: you can ONLY include 10 things. Choose carefully!"
3. Children draw silently for 5 minutes.
4. Share: pairs compare maps. Did you choose the same 10 things? Why did you include the door? Why did you leave out the plant?
5. Class votes: which 10 features are MOST important for someone who has never been in this room?
6. Create a "Class Consensus Map" on the whiteboard with the agreed 10 features.

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"Pixel's brain is small — she can only remember what MATTERS. That's why cats are such good hunters — they abstract away everything except the mouse!"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Provide a partially completed map template with 5 features; children add 5 more.
- Extension: Create TWO maps — one for "finding your seat" (desks important) and one for "fire exit route" (doors/windows important).

**Safety/Practical Notes:**
None beyond standard classroom supervision.

### Lesson 2.4 — Computational Thinking in Action
**Activity Name:** Pixel's Beach Trip Planning Board
**Format:** Hybrid (Group Challenge + Craft)

**Group Size:** Small groups of 4
**Placement in Lesson:** Consolidation (alongside "Plan Pixel's Adventure" platform activity)

**Materials Needed:**
- Large poster paper (1 per group)
- 4 section dividers per poster (labelled: DECOMPOSITION, PATTERNS, ABSTRACTION, ALGORITHM)
- Sticky notes (4 colours, one per pillar)
- Marker pens
- Timer

**Learning Connection:**
This applies ALL FOUR computational thinking pillars to one real problem (Pixel travelling from school to beach), creating a visual artefact that demonstrates each pillar. It directly supports the platform project.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Each group gets a poster divided into 4 quadrants labelled with the pillars.
2. Challenge: Plan Pixel's beach trip by filling in each quadrant.
3. Round 1 — DECOMPOSITION (3 min): Break the trip into sub-tasks (pack bag, travel there, play, travel home, etc.)

6. Round 4 — ALGORITHM (4 min): Write a step-by-step sequence using LOOPS and IF/THEN where possible.
7. Groups present in 1 minute each, pointing to each quadrant.

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"Pixel uses ALL FOUR superpowers together — that's what makes her a Computational Thinking CAT-ion! (Get it?)"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Provide prompt questions on each quadrant.
- Extension: Add a "Bug" — what could go wrong? — and a "Debug" response.

**Safety/Practical Notes:**
None beyond standard classroom supervision.

### Lesson 3.1 — Welcome to Block Code!

**Placement in Lesson:** Hook (before first platform coding)

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"Sparky's code blocks got scattered by a lightning bolt! Help Sparky rebuild the program!"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Colour-code the blocks so the snap order is visually obvious (red → orange → yellow → green).

### Lesson 3.2 — Movement and Direction
**Activity Name:** Human Sprite Square Dance
**Format:** Music & Movement / Outdoor
**Time:** 15 minutes
**Group Size:** Whole class
**Placement in Lesson:** Between platform activities (after 3.2.A Draw a Square, before 3.2.B Maze Navigator)

**Materials Needed:**
- Playground/hall space
- Chalk or tape to mark starting positions
- Large angle cards: 90°, 120°, 60°
- Teacher as "Programmer" with the following script visible

**Learning Connection:**
This physically reinforces the exact block code: `Repeat 4 { Move 100 steps, Turn right 90 degrees }` by having children BE the sprite. It directly teaches the maths connection: 360 ÷ sides = turn angle.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Children stand in a grid formation (marked spots), all facing the same direction.
2. Teacher calls: "WHEN I SAY GO... REPEAT 4 TIMES: Walk 10 steps forward, Turn right 90 degrees. GO!"

4. Discuss: "We turned 90 degrees 4 times. 90 × 4 = 360. A full circle! That's why we ended up facing the same way."
5. Triangle challenge: "How many degrees to turn for a triangle?" (120) "How many repeats?" (3) — try it.
6. Hexagon challenge: (60 degrees, 6 repeats) — try it.

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"You're all Sparkys now! When Sparky codes 'Turn right 90 degrees,' Sparky does exactly what you just did. Can you feel how 90 degrees is a sharp corner, but 60 degrees is gentler?"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Walk alongside struggling children; use arm gestures to show turn direction.
- Extension: Create a pentagon (72 degrees, 5 repeats) — predict, then test.

**Safety/Practical Notes:**
Use a hall if weather is poor; ensure adequate space between children to avoid collisions.

### Lesson 3.3 — Events and Interactions
**Activity Name:** Event Trigger Relay
**Format:** Physical Game
**Time:** 12 minutes
**Group Size:** Teams of 6
**Placement in Lesson:** Hook (before 3.3.A Sparky's Dance Party)

**Materials Needed:**
- Large "Event" cards: "When [up arrow] pressed", "When [space] pressed", "When this sprite clicked", "When I receive [dance]"
- Large "Action" cards: "Move up 10 steps", "Play sound cheer", "Say I was clicked!", "Start dancing"
- Tape to mark "coding area" on the floor

**Learning Connection:**
This pre-teaches the concept that EVENTS (keyboard presses, clicks, broadcasts) TRIGGER ACTIONS. Children physically experience: "Nothing happens until an event occurs" — crucial for understanding event-driven programming in 3.3.A.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Each team of 6 divides into 3 Event children and 3 Action children.
2. Event children stand at "EVENT LINE" (one end of the room); Action children stand at "SCRIPTS AREA" (middle).
3. Teacher holds up an Event card (e.g., "When [space] pressed").
4. The Event child holding the matching card runs to an Action child and tags them.
5. That Action child performs their action (e.g., jumps up and down if their card is "Jump 10 times").
6. Key rule: Action children CANNOT move until tagged by an Event. This teaches: "Code doesn't run until triggered!"
7. Speed round: Teacher rapid-fires event cards.

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"Sparky waits for events all day — 'When flag clicked... When key pressed...' Now YOU feel what it's like to wait for your event!"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Use only 2 event types and 2 action types.
- Extension: Introduce "When I receive [message]" — one child shouts "DANCE TIME!" and all Action children with dance cards respond.

**Safety/Practical Notes:**
Ensure running lanes are clear; no tagging faces.

**Materials Needed:**

### Lesson 6.3 — How Computers Store Information
**Activity Name:** RAM vs Storage Bucket Relay
**Format:** Physical Game
**Time:** 12 minutes
**Group Size:** Two teams
**Placement in Lesson:** Hook (before RAM vs Storage discussion)

**Materials Needed:**
- 2 "RAM" buckets (with holes drilled in the bottom!)
- 2 "STORAGE" buckets (solid, no holes)
- Water (or dried beans/rice to avoid mess)
- Measuring cups
- Timer

**Learning Connection:**
This dramatically demonstrates the difference between **RAM** (fast but temporary — leaks when "power" is off) and **Storage** (permanent — holds data even when "power" is off).

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**

4. Now race to fill the STORAGE bucket (solid). Time stops when full.
5. Turn buckets upside down — RAM is empty, STORAGE still has water. "Storage keeps data even when power is off!"
6. Discussion: "Why do we need RAM if it forgets everything?" (Speed! It's faster to access.)

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"Chip stores important memories in STORAGE so they're never lost. But Chip works in RAM because it's FAST!"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Use dried beans instead of water to reduce mess/risk.
- Extension: Calculate how long RAM "lasts" under different pour rates.

**Safety/Practical Notes:**
Use a waterproof area or beans; ensure no slipping hazards.

**Format:** Physical Game / Outdoor
**Time:** 15 minutes
**Group Size:** Whole class
**Placement in Lesson:** Hook (before network discussion)

**Materials Needed:**

**Learning Connection:**

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**

6. CUT one router's connection — can messages still get through? (Yes, via a different path!)

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"Chip travels along networks like this — from your computer, to a router, to the BIG INTERNET, and back! If one path is blocked, Chip finds another way."

### Lesson 10.2 — Functions: Reusable Code Blocks (Part 1)
**Activity Name:** Custom Block Assembly Line
**Format:** Physical Game / Role-Play
**Time:** 15 minutes
**Group Size:** Groups of 6
**Placement in Lesson:** Hook (before Define custom block platform activity)

- Large "action" cards: "Move 100 steps", "Turn right 90 degrees"
- Large "DEFINE" header cards: "Draw Square"
- Large "CALL" cards
- Assembly line ropes/taped paths

**Learning Connection:**
This teaches FUNCTIONS (reusable code blocks) by having children physically create and CALL custom actions. Once "Draw Square" is DEFINED, any child can CALL it instead of repeating 8 actions.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**

5. Discuss: "Which was easier? Calling 8 times or calling once?"
6. Create a "Draw Triangle" function card.

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"Logic is LAZY — in the best way! Why say something 8 times when you can say it ONCE and reuse it forever? That's FUNCTIONS!"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Use only 4-action functions (move, turn, move, turn).
- Extension: Create a function that CALLS another function (nested).

**Safety/Practical Notes:**
Ensure clear paths for physical movement; no running.

### Lesson 10.4 — Advanced Game Project: Movement and Gravity
**Activity Name:** Paper Prototype Gravity Jump
**Format:** Craft / Design
**Time:** 15 minutes
**Group Size:** Pairs
**Placement in Lesson:** Planning phase (before coding movement and gravity)

**Materials Needed:**
- Graph paper (for "pixel" accuracy)
- Character cutouts that can be moved across the paper
- Paper strips to represent VELOCITY
- Ruler for measuring "jumps"

**Learning Connection:**
This paper prototypes the gravity physics before coding, helping children understand Y_VELOCITY changing by -1 each frame (falling) and the upward impulse on jump.

**Step-by-Step Instructions:**
1. Draw a simple platform level on graph paper.
2. Place character cutout on a platform.
3. Simulate FALLING: move character DOWN one square, then 2, then 3, then 4 (accelerating!) until hitting ground.
4. Simulate JUMP: move character UP 5 squares suddenly, then apply falling: 4, 3, 2, 1, 0, -1, -2... until landing.
5. Mark each position with a dot. Connect dots to show the JUMP ARC.
6. Discuss: "What would happen if we changed Y_VELOCITY by -2 instead of -1?" (Faster falling!)

**Mascot Tie-In:**
"Logic understands PHYSICS. Gravity pulls DOWN constantly. Jumping pushes UP once. This creates the jump arc you see in every platform game!"

**Differentiation:**
- Support: Pre-drawn jump arc to trace.
- Extension: Model double-jump physics (second upward impulse mid-air).

**Safety/Practical Notes:**
None beyond standard classroom supervision.
